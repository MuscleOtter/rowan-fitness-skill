## 1.16.0 — 2026-09-16 (main; release not published)

Adds Theo, a habits and follow-through coach (`habits`), after a tester's full plan showed that an approved plan still needs help getting started. Theo is not a reviewer and never changes approved actions. After a first plan is released, Theo offers a skippable handoff, with a quick version: up to two when-and-where anchors tied to existing routines, one or two ways to make it easier, reminders through the athlete's own apps where the assistant can reach them, a version-labeled workout card that pastes the approved plan text unchanged and is checked line by line before sending, a log sheet with the same header, and a dated first check-in. Rowan does this work in Theo's voice; no separate agent is needed. At check-ins, a habit loop works on one habit at a time: notice what got in the way, adjust one thing, track simply, then keep, adjust, stop or leave pending, with missing entries treated as unknown and a stopped tactic's reason carried forward. Missed sessions are information, skipping for pain or illness is the right call, there are no penalties or streak pressure, and Theo never adds make-up sessions. The general pain escalation rule now asks for clinician input in the same reply, not after waiting or trying the exercise again, and forbids calling other exercises safe for the painful area; a held part stays held until a reviewed revision releases it, since feeling better is not a release. Any new food, sleep or training habit returns to review. Theo agrees times only after checking sleep, caffeine, fueling, spacing and medication conditions, checks spacing against the last session before naming a first session, and moves a session to another day only under a single session-move rule that keeps order, spacing, weekly dose and conditions; without stated spacing, a move that newly stacks sessions returns to review. Revisions, pauses, suspensions and reversals name affected materials and reminders instead of repeating the handoff; a new version is explained with its own “What changed and why” list, replaces old cards and sheets, keeps when-and-where plans and reminders that still fit, and gets new ones only for changed pieces. An approved alternative containing a held exercise is quoted unchanged, then the held exercise is named. Unreviewed programs get the same help, labeled as not reviewed by Rowan. Before a first plan or big change, Rowan considers phasing new behaviors in, in Theo's role, so the phase-in is reviewed with the plan.

The design draws on implementation-intention and habit-formation research, confirmed in Crossref, and on ideas from *Atomic Habits* and *Tiny Habits*, paraphrased with no text or templates copied. Penalty contracts, streak pressure, hustle slogans and unsourced statistics were rejected. The Training Record schema becomes FRB-state-1.9 with optional handoff and habit-loop fields. Twenty-six verification cases are added, the habit research list lives in feature sources, and the README, How It Works, FAQ, Setup, Share, Discovery, llms.txt, citation and Validation pages are updated.

The plan and the build each received an adversarial self-review by the authoring AI model. Independent maintenance review under rubric v1.2 is in progress. Pass 1 failed (weighted 8.694; one material and eleven minor findings). Pass 2 failed (weighted 8.926; no material and five new minor findings, with verification at 8.7 because no run of the revised candidate had been observed). Pass 3 met the release rule narrowly (weighted 8.869; four dimensions at the 8.8 floor; no material and ten minor findings), with evidence from four fictional runs on its candidate. Across the three candidates, fresh evaluators of fictional single-reply runs found reworded workout cards, a paused exercise that could resume on the athlete's word, clinician input deferred, loosely quoted short-session options, missing review routes and missing spacing checks; card copying, holds and spacing improved, while clinician timing, quoting alternatives and review routes kept recurring. Pass 3 recommended moving rules to their canonical homes and shrinking the habits reference rather than adding wording; this candidate does that (about 2,020 to 1,590 words) and addresses its findings. The exact-final check and runs on this candidate have not been completed. Nothing here has been exercised on a live host.

## 1.15.0 — 2026-09-16 (unreleased preparation)

Adds two review depths (review depth policy v1). A **standard review** (one critique pass by every required specialist, a revision and exact-final verification) becomes the default for lower-risk recommendations and revisions, at about half the reviewer calls. A **full review** keeps all three passes and is required for medications, diagnosed conditions, pregnancy or postpartum status, clinician restrictions, pain or injury, cuts and other weight-change plans, beginners or returners with health risks, new high-intensity work or large load jumps, and whenever the athlete asks. A standard review escalates to full after a substantive final failure or confirmed open material finding on D1 with unchanged inputs and roles. Technical report failures use bounded retries; changed dependencies or missing expertise require a complete rebuilt cycle, with full depth when triggered. Rowan chooses conservatively, records the reason and labels each release with its actual depth; the athlete can raise the depth but never lower it. Both depths keep the same roles, rubric, score floors, independence and binding rules.

This formalizes what a tester's Rowan improvised as an undefined one-pass review. The README, How It Works, FAQ, Share and llms.txt pages now describe both depths. Changing stage counts is an evaluated amendment under the project's rules; the initial independent v1.1 evaluation failed. The user authorized a prospective v1.2 rubric amendment before a new sequence. Local consistency fixes align host and maintenance dispatch, clarify retries/escalation/restarts, preserve depth history, and shorten the discovery description. Separate-context maintenance critiques and bounded fictional routing probes are recorded privately. A fictional standard case completed all six fresh role reviews and release checks, including saved-artifact continuation after host capacity failures. The separate full case completed all twelve role reviews and release checks after resuming unchanged packets following an account usage interruption. Both are bounded synthetic workflows with documented frozen-source applicability; no athlete outcomes, clinical equivalence or measured savings are claimed.

## 1.14.0 — 2026-09-16 (unreleased preparation)

Fixes problems a tester hit in a full first plan. Changes made after the athlete's feedback are now new candidates reviewed before release: in the tester's session, a load-finding method, a new hanging progression, caffeine and supplement advice and a medication-related caution were added after release as “within approved scope” without review, while the plan still read as board-cleared. Feedback is gathered into one revision before review. Until it passes, the last approved version stays active except for parts affected by new facts such as pain, which pause at once, and warnings that only say to stop or seek medical care are never held back. Plans now carry visible versions (Plan v1, v2) with their actual review status, a short “What changed and why” list and a way back to an earlier version when it was properly reviewed and its conditions still hold. Rowan also may not invent a lighter review or let the athlete choose one; in that session the athlete was offered a one-pass “standard” review that the rules do not define.

Plans now find a starting load before progressing it. The tester's plan jumped to what to do “once 25 kg feels easy”, using the heaviest dumbbell from the equipment list as a progression rule before any working load existed, with load steps never confirmed. New lifters find that load from two or three sets of smooth reps rather than reps-in-reserve estimates they cannot yet make. Unless the program defines its own progression, progression adds reps, then the smallest real load step; tempo, pauses, variations and bands are for when load cannot rise, and Kit's equipment facts bound progression without setting it.

Nutrition moves up front. Sage joins every full weekly/program review (routing policy v3). Rowan asks once whether the athlete wants numbers, portions or no nutrition coaching; with coaching, full plans put nutrition near the top with something usable from the start, such as a starting energy range with its method and uncertainty and a protein target. Unknown intake is estimated from app history or a recall and needs from weight trend or an equation, with a short log refining rather than delaying guidance. Appetite-suppressing medication shifts the emphasis to energy and protein floors with the prescriber involved, and numbers are never pushed on someone who declines them or has shared a history of disordered eating. Rowan can offer a nutrition-focused session after the first plan, with Sage reviewing any advice from it. Setup now asks adults about alcohol and nicotine once, kindly and with the reason, and the no-shame rule names them explicitly.

Also corrects parts of 1.13.0 found in an adversarial review: follow-up probing digs freely into training history but raises injuries, past diets and other health history only when a plan decision needs them; receipts can say health details were found without reciting them and raise safety conflicts plainly; the date rule confirms the day when no tool can work out the weekday and follows the athlete's date style; the greeting calls its time estimate rough, allows for longer intakes and has a fresh-start version for beginners; and the opening tracking question asks one thing. The package check now explains that source downloads omit `dist/`.

The README, How It Works, FAQ, Setup, llms.txt and Validation pages now describe these changes. A link check of 412 internal links and 109 external links found one dead source, ACSM's exercise-intensity infographic, which now points to its new address.

None of these changes has been exercised on a live host. Review depth itself is unchanged: every new or revised recommendation still requires three critique/revision passes and exact-final verification.

## 1.5.1 — 2026-09-09

Corrects defects an independent review found in the 1.5.0 host layer. 1.5.0 was merged but never released, so no published package carried them. Coaching policy, roles, rubric and thresholds remain unchanged.

- **ChatGPT had no working route.** The 1.5.0 instructions supplied a starter prompt and `athlete.md` but never the skill's own files, so a session had Rowan's name and none of Rowan's rules. - **Binding mode no longer keys off shell persistence.** Any code execution that can read the frozen payload can compute a real SHA-256; persistence is a separate question. Chat surfaces can therefore hash and compare instead of falling back to manual attestation.
- **Inherited-context delegation is now excluded explicitly.** A fork or continuation worker carries the author's deliberation, so its report can be correctly bound and still be self-review. Reviewers must start from an empty context; if a session offers only an inheriting mode, the board is unavailable.
